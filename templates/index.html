Prompts

Your SillyTavern prompt (preset, character card, persona, lore, Post-History Instructions) is what tells Claude how to write. The bridge adds only a short frame explaining how SillyTavern's messages are laid out, plus the optional style notes and the Creativity text below.

Style Notes optional

How This Works

  • System prompt: the bridge frame, your style notes (if any), SillyTavern's prompt, the Creativity mode, and tool definitions. It stays fixed for a CLI session, which keeps Claude's earlier thinking valid and the prompt cache warm.
  • Chat: a new session gets the chat so far as a transcript; later turns send only your new message. Lore or notes that SillyTavern adds or changes mid-session are added to that turn, and Post-History Instructions are re-sent when they change.
  • Thinking: Claude always thinks before replying; the Effort setting controls how much. No prompt asks it to write its reasoning out. With "Include Thinking" on, its thinking summary goes to SillyTavern's reasoning block.

Character Memory

A character remembers another chat only where you ask for it: draw a connection from one character or group chat to another and pick the chats, or bind the character to a persona so they remember the chats with that persona you pick, and every new one. Sonnet recaps those chats from each character's point of view where the character is in them, and as a plain account where they aren't. Memories count over what the card says about the character's history. How it works is below.

Persona bindings

A bound character remembers, in each chat with that persona, the chats you tick under it, plus every new chat with the persona from the day you bind (branches only if you tick them). A chat never brings in itself or its own branches.

Chats

The character's own chats and their group chats, each with the chats it's connected to. Open one to read or edit its recap, or to connect chats to it.

What other chats get

A chat remembered in another chat comes either as one synopsis or as its chapters. Each connection and each binding has its own choice: pick it when you connect a chat (or with Edit on the connection) and in each binding. The choice here is the default for new ones. In chapters mode Sonnet divides a chat of three parts or more (81 messages and up) by event, at the scene breaks its recaps mark, and each connection or binding can leave chapters out: open its recap to choose. Both are written from the chat's scene recaps, and scene recaps already shorter than the target are passed on as they are.

How it works
The graph
Each dot is a character (circle), a group chat (square) or a persona (diamond). A thin line joins a group to each member. A dashed line joins a persona to a character bound to it. A thick line with arrows is memory: the arrow points at the one who remembers, so a line with two arrows is mutual. It stands for every connection between their chats, branches included. At first the graph shows only what has memory or a binding, and their neighbours. Show all shows everything, the chips hide a kind, and the search box finds any node. Drag to move around, pinch or scroll to zoom, and drag a dot to move it. Tap a dot or a line to open it.
Setup
Turn on Character Memory in Settings, and install the bridge's SillyTavern extension, Bridge Memory: run st-extension/install.sh from the bridge's folder and reload SillyTavern. The extension adds which chat, persona and character each request is for, which nothing else in a request says. Memory skips turns without it. The status at the top of this tab says whether it has been heard from.
Recaps
The bridge reads a chat's file from SillyTavern's folder, and Sonnet recaps it 40 messages at a time, each part with the previous part's recap for context. A part the character has a message in is recapped from their point of view. A part they have no message in is recapped as a plain account of what happened, which never puts them in it, and every character who isn't in that part shares the one account. Hidden and empty messages are left out. A recap is written scene by scene, each paragraph opening with the number of the message its scene starts at, like [#52]; the model reads it without those marks. Recaps are built in the background when a chat that needs them is used or opened in SillyTavern, or when you ask here, and a reply uses whatever is ready; the rest arrives on later replies. A part is recapped again only when its messages change, and a branch shares its parent's recaps up to the branch point. Open a chat from a character's panel to read what the character remembers of it; Recap beside a connected chat, or a chat in a binding, shows it in place. Edit lets you rewrite any part, the synopsis or a chapter; Redo has it written again. An edit of a synopsis or chapter lasts until a part it's written from changes.
Synopsis or chapters
Other chats don't get a chat's part recaps but what's written from them, chosen for each connection (when you connect a chat, or with Edit) and each binding (its Other chats get each chat as switch); the choice at the top of this tab is the default for new ones. Synopsis: one recap of the whole chat in about the number of words you set. Chapters: Sonnet divides a chat of three parts or more (81 messages and up) into chapters by event, at the scene breaks its recaps mark, so a chapter can start in the middle of a part, once for every character, titles them, and writes each in about the number of words you set. A chat too short for chapters comes as its synopsis. When the chat grows, only its last chapter is divided again, so the earlier ones keep their place and what you chose for them. Untick chapters per connection (in its recap on the memory line, or in the chat) and per binding; a binding's choice holds in all its chats. Scene recaps already within the word target go as they are, with no call. A chat one connection gets as a synopsis and another as chapters has both written from the same part recaps. Switching a connection or binding to the other mode, or changing a word target, has each chat it affects written that way the next time it's needed; a division into chapters, once made, is kept.
Earlier in a chat
When SillyTavern leaves a chat's oldest messages out to fit the context, the recap of those messages comes in instead.
Connections
Tap Connect, then the character or group whose chats should be remembered, then the one that remembers them (or Connect to… on a character). Pick the chat that's remembered and the chat that remembers it, who remembers it, and what it gets, a synopsis or chapters. A chat's dialog connects chats to it too. In a one-character chat, the character remembers it. In a group chat it's everyone in it, members who join later included, or only the members you tick, so a private scene can stay with the one character who was in it. A character who isn't in the remembered chat at all gets it as background: the memory says they weren't there and know only what they'd have heard or been told. In a group chat, Leave out for in one member's view of the chat keeps a connection for everyone from that member.
Directions and mirroring
Tap a memory line to see its connections and pick its direction: one way, the other way, or Mutual. Mutual adds, for every connection, its mirror: the chat that remembered is remembered back, for everyone in it, passed on the same way. One way turns the connections going the other way round, and Remove disconnects them all. The dashboard says first how many Sonnet calls recapping the new ones takes.
Persona bindings
Bind a character to a persona, then tick the chats with that persona they should remember; Select all ticks every chat with it. Each binding passes its chats on as a synopsis or as chapters, its own choice. From then on each new chat with that character and persona joins by itself. Branches don't, as they're alternate timelines; tick them if you want them. In every chat with the persona, the character remembers all the ticked chats except the chat itself and its own branches. A chat's Don't bring in through bindings keeps it out everywhere; Leave out here keeps one chat's memory out of one other chat.
What the model gets
A new CLI session gets a <character_memory> block before the transcript: the recap of this chat's earlier part, then the other chats' synopses or chapters, oldest first, each saying who was there, or that the character wasn't and it's background. Newest chats get theirs while 24,000 characters last; a chat whose chapters don't all fit loses its earliest ones, and older chats that don't fit are left out. After that, each reply carries only recaps that are new or changed, and a note when one is dropped. The block says the memories count over the card's description and scenario about the character's history and relationships. Only the character whose reply it is gets their memories; in a group chat each member has their own CLI session.
Conflicts
Once a chat with memories from other chats has your first message, Sonnet compares how it starts (its greeting, your first message and its Author's Note) with the memories of chats the character was in. Background from chats they weren't in isn't checked. If they clash, say the chat has them meet as strangers or makes them siblings, SillyTavern shows it after the next reply: keep the memories, or untick the ones to leave out of this chat. The chat here shows it too.
Cards
A character can have several cards, such as the same person on cards from different creators. Add the others to the character, and their chats and memories become one character's. Removing a card takes its chats back with it.
Cost
Nothing runs per reply. Recapping takes one Sonnet call per 40 messages, once, plus one per 40 messages for the plain account of parts a character isn't in, shared by everyone who isn't. A connection for everyone in a group chat is recapped for each member, so the dashboard asks first with an estimate. A synopsis takes one more, chapters one to divide the chat plus one per chapter (none for a synopsis or chapter short enough already), and a conflict check one. They count against your plan like any other call and are listed on the History tab under Character Memory.
